# U of T MEng in Mechanical & Industrial Engineering

Research brief for applying from a UX degree + statistics minor. Sources are official MIE, Faculty of Applied Science & Engineering, and School of Graduate Studies pages (2026–27 cycle). Use this as application north-star material: the letter of intent, areas-of-interest checkboxes, and technical-emphasis picks should all tell the same story.

**Recommended application selections**

- **Areas of interest (internal, optional):** Data Science / AI / ML; Human Factors; Healthcare Engineering; Information Engineering.
- **Technical emphases (max two; appear on transcript if completed):** Data Analytics & Machine Learning; Entrepreneurship, Leadership, Innovation & Technology in Engineering (ELITE).

Healthcare Engineering and Information Engineering are **certificates**, not transcript emphases. They still belong in the interest checkboxes and in the letter, because they describe the industrial-engineering home for human-centered, data-driven work.

---

## What they emphasize

MIE’s professional master’s is built to make people **competitive for industry jobs**, not to train academic researchers. The department’s own comparison is blunt: MEng is course-based, customizable (10 courses from 100+), specialization through emphases, optional faculty-supervised project and optional co-op/practicum. MASc/PhD are the research stream.

The story they sell:

1. **Flexibility as the product.** Duration is chosen at application: full-time (about 12 months), extended full-time (16–24 months), or part-time (28–36 months). You can also add a project (replaces three courses) and, starting Fall 2027, a limited-enrolment 8-month paid co-op.
2. **Industrial engineering as a broad professional toolkit.** SGS describes IE research at MIE as **human factors, information engineering, operations research, and applied machine learning**. That is the side of the department this application should claim. Mechanical-side areas (robotics, mechanics & design, materials, thermofluids) exist, but they are not the fit.
3. **Data, AI, and decision systems as the current brand.** The graduate landing page leads with “Artificial Intelligence and Optimization… Robotics, Information Engineering and Advanced Manufacturing.” Associate Chair of Professional Programs Samin Aref is an industrial engineer / computer scientist who teaches in the Data Analytics & Machine Learning emphasis.
4. **Industry-connected, not isolated coursework.** Faculty research is often industry-sponsored. MEng students can get industry-shaped experience by doing a project in a lab, not only by taking classes. AMIGAS and Faculty-wide events are framed as networking infrastructure.
5. **Non-traditional undergrads are expected.** MIE says many professors themselves trained outside ME/IE (computer science, psychology, mathematics, other fields). If the graduate courses, emphases, or research interest you, “one of our programs is suitable.” Many current grad students did not study mechanical or industrial engineering as undergrads. That is official, not informal advice.

The calendar line that matters for tone: the MEng “is designed for students preparing for **advanced professional activity**; it is not a research-oriented degree.” The letter should sound like a professional plan (courses, emphases, target roles), not a PhD proposal.

## What they teach

### Degree structure

Complete **5.0 full-course equivalents (FCEs)**:

| Path | Requirements |
| --- | --- |
| Coursework only | 10 graduate half-courses. At least 6 of 10 must be “technical” (MIE courses or the approved APS technical list). |
| Coursework + project | 7 half-courses (at least 4 of 7 technical) + `MIE8888Y` / `APS1803Y` project (1.5 FCE). Opt in no later than the term of the seventh course. |

Three course failures (below B− / 70%) trigger a recommendation for termination. Transfer credit is possible for up to two unused prior courses (B+ or higher), requested in the first session.

**Delivery options (chosen on the application)**

- **Full-time:** up to 5 half-courses per session; can finish in three sessions; max 3 years. International applicants are steered toward extended full-time because program length affects post-grad work-permit length.
- **Extended full-time:** two-year fee; max 7 half-courses per year and 3 per session. Cannot transfer in or out (except co-op → EFT).
- **Part-time:** max 2 half-courses per session, 4 per 12 months; up to 6 years.

Summer MIE graduate courses are scarce. Plan required MIE courses in fall/winter; use summer for ELITE / APS / other Faculty courses.

**Co-op (new, Fall 2027, limited seats)**

Apply as MEng Extended Full-Time + Co-op by **February 8, 2027**. Mandatory `TEP1701H` (Navigating Engineering Workplaces) in Fall Year 1; that course can also count toward ELITE. Mandatory 8-month paid work term `TEP1702Y` (May–December 2028). Average cited pay is about $5,000 CAD/month (based on undergrad PEY). Work terms are **not guaranteed**; unsuccessful students continue as regular EFT. Co-op admissions look for a strong academic record and “eagerness to succeed on the job,” and may include an interview. If rejected from co-op, you are automatically considered for regular EFT.

### Industrial engineering curriculum areas (what you would actually take)

These are the department’s IE course families, from the 2026–27 graduate schedule and course descriptions.

**Information Engineering / data science (the technical spine)**

| Course | What it is | Why it matters here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `APS1070H` Foundations of Data Analytics and Machine Learning | Python, probability/stats, linear algebra, algorithms, optimization. Prerequisite for the DAML emphasis. Taught in part by Samin Aref. | First-term technical leveling course. A stats minor helps; a UX degree does not waive it unless you have multiple A-level ML/CS courses. |
| `MIE1626H` Data Science Methods and Statistical Learning | Statistical foundations of data science: cleaning, EDA, inference, resampling, regression, trees, SVMs. Taught by Aref. | Strongest core match to a stats minor. Exclusion: `MIE1624H`. |
| `MIE1624H` Intro to Data Science, Analytics and AI | Applied analytics in Python (and IBM Watson): regression, simulation, ML, text, visualization, case studies in finance, healthcare, marketing, smart cities. | Broader and more tool-oriented than 1626. Do **not** take both. Prefer 1626 if you want statistical depth; 1624 if you want a survey + applications. |
| `MIE1517H` Introduction to Deep Learning | CNNs, RNNs, regularization, transfer learning, generative models; ethics/fairness; team project. | Natural next step after APS1070 if the target roles are ML-adjacent. |
| `MIE1513H` Decision Support Systems | Role of DSS in organizations: retrieval, descriptive/predictive modeling, recommenders, visualization. | Connects data work to organizational decisions — the IE version of “analytics that someone actually uses.” |
| `MIE1628H` Cloud-Based Data Analytics | Hadoop/Spark, Azure/AWS/GCP, structured and unstructured data, real-time analytics, ML on the cloud. | Directly relevant to data engineer / MLOps-adjacent titles they list for alumni. |
| `MIE1512H` Data Analytics | Research-seminar style; large-scale / web data management; reproduce published analytics work. | Heavier and more research-flavored; better as an elective than a first data course. |
| `MIE524H` Data Mining | Scalable ML on large datasets (MapReduce/Spark), graphs, streams. | Technical elective if you already have programming comfort. |
| `MIE1501H` Knowledge Modelling and Management | Semantic Web, RDF, ontologies, OWL. | Core of the Information Engineering *certificate*; only take it if you mean that certificate. |

**Human Factors (the UX → IE translation)**

This is the department’s official language for human-centered systems. It is not “UX bootcamp”; it is engineering of human–machine systems, with methods that look familiar (task analysis, experimental design, qualitative field work, stats on behavioral data).

| Course | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `MIE1401H` Human Factors Engineering | Human information processing, decision making, workload, error; human-centered design process: task analysis, requirements, prototyping, evaluation; HCI, displays/controls, training. |
| `MIE1413H` Statistical Models in Empirical Research | Regression, mixed models, GLMs, time series, clustering on observational/experimental (often HF) data. Requires basic probability, stats, experimental design. Taught by Birsen Donmez. |
| `MIE1416H` Human Factors in Healthcare | HF applied to Canadian/US healthcare systems; prospective/retrospective safety; **integration of technology including ML/AI in clinical environments**; equity, patient engagement. |
| `MIE1412H` Human-Automation Interaction | Trust, workload, situation awareness, adaptive/intelligent automation, supervisory control. |
| `MIE1402H` / `MIE1403H` | Experimental and analytical methods in HF research. |
| `MIE1499H` (2026–27) | Qualitative field methods: interviews, observation, focus groups, artifact analysis. |
| `MIE542H` Human Factors Integration | Putting HF into real engineering projects (energy, healthcare, military, software). |
| `MIE523H` Engineering Psychology and Human Performance | Perception, attention, memory, decision making; human–automation in control rooms. |

Labs that sit under this work: Human Factors and Applied Statistics (Donmez), Cognitive Engineering (Jamieson), Safety, Equity & Design (Alfred), Interactive Media Lab (Chignell), Psychology-Informed Engineering Design (Shu).

**Healthcare Engineering (application domain, not a transcript emphasis)**

Framed as using engineers to cut wait times, improve scheduling and information access, and plan capacity. Certificate core: `MIE561` Healthcare Systems (real hospital/system case studies; open to non-IE students), `MIE1623` Introduction to Healthcare Engineering (OR methods on healthcare datasets), `APS1001` Project Management. Electives include HF methods, scheduling, simulation, and knowledge modeling. A project/internship at a health organization can replace three electives.

**ELITE (the business/leadership layer)**

Four half-courses from any mix of:

- Leadership (`TEP1010`, `TEP1011`, `TEP1026`, `TEP1501`, …)
- Entrepreneurship & Innovation (`APS1012`, `APS1013`, `APS1023`, `APS1088`, …)
- Finance & Management (`APS502`, `APS1001`, `APS1016`, `APS1017`, `APS1052` AI in Finance, …)
- Engineering & Society (`APS510`, `APS1024`, `APS1090`, …)

`APS1070` does **not** count toward ELITE. One course may double-count across two emphases. You cannot earn more than two emphases.

### Emphases vs certificates (do not mix these up on the form)

**Transcript emphases (pick at most two on the application):** Advanced Manufacturing; Advanced Soft Materials; Advanced Water Technologies; Biomanufacturing; **Data Analytics & Machine Learning**; Engineering and Globalization; **ELITE**; Forensic Engineering; Identity, Privacy and Security; Robotics; Sustainable Energy; Waterpower.

**MIE certificates (separate paper certificate, not on the transcript):** Computational Mechanics in Design; Financial Engineering; **Healthcare Engineering**; **Information Engineering**. Only one course may overlap between two certificates/emphases.

Identity, Privacy and Security is hosted by ECE; MIE students can pursue it, but it is a weaker fit than DAML + ELITE.

### A 10-course plan that matches this application

This is a sketch for the letter (“courses I intend to take”), not a commitment. Majority-technical rule is satisfied.

1. `APS1070H` — DAML prerequisite
2. `MIE1626H` — DAML core (statistical learning)
3. `MIE1517H` or `MIE1513H` — DAML elective (deep learning **or** decision support)
4. `MIE1628H` or `MIE1413H` — cloud analytics **or** statistical models for empirical/HF data
5. `MIE1401H` — human factors engineering
6. `MIE1416H` or `MIE561H` — healthcare as the application domain
7. `APS1001H` Project Management — ELITE (Finance & Management) and Healthcare certificate core
8. One more ELITE course (leadership, entrepreneurship, or engineering & society)
9. One more ELITE or DAML elective
10. Open slot: `MIE1412H` (human–automation), `MIE1623H` (healthcare engineering), or a project instead of three electives

DAML math: `APS1070` + one core (`1626`) + three electives from the DAML list. `MIE1413`, `MIE1513`, `MIE1517`, `MIE1623`, and `MIE1628` are all on that elective list, so human-factors stats and healthcare can count toward DAML.

If a supervised project is used, cut three electives and write the project as applied work in a HF, healthcare, or information-engineering lab. Faculty research is often industry-sponsored; that is the department’s own suggested path to “industry experience.”

## What graduates do

MIE states that an MIE education is “extremely adaptable career-wise.” Alumni titles they list explicitly:

- Business intelligence analyst
- Data scientist / senior data analyst
- DevOps / MLOps
- Healthcare analyst
- Machine learning scientist
- R&D consultant
- Financial analyst
- Founders / own businesses

Sectors named: finance, government, healthcare, manufacturing, plus engineering roles in ME/IE industries.

**DAML emphasis alumni** (Faculty page): banking, government services, finance, healthcare, manufacturing. Common first roles: BI analyst, data engineer, data scientist, DevOps/MLOps engineer, machine learning engineer, R&D consultant, senior data analyst. The Faculty’s line is that DAML graduates are “among the most sought out engineers with AI, Data Science, and Information Engineering skills in the local and the North American job market.” Applications they name include personalized e-commerce, logistics, digital marketing, **adaptive user interfaces**, and health/medical imaging.

**ELITE emphasis alumni:** aerospace, construction, consulting, environmental services, manufacturing, mining, transportation. First roles include business systems engineer, **data scientist**, engineering analyst, management consultant, project specialist, **product manager**.

That last pair — data scientist and product manager — is why ELITE is the second emphasis, not a distraction. The department already treats business, leadership, and product-shaped roles as legitimate MEng outcomes.

Student-facing language on the graduate site: events, faculty/industry connections, and lab projects as the practical path, not only lectures.

## What they look for in applicants

### Formal bar

- Bachelor’s degree with “high academic standing” from a recognized university. **Not required to be ME or IE.**
- **Minimum** GPA: 3.0 / mid-B / 73–76% in the **final two years**. Meeting the minimum does not guarantee admission; the office will not pre-assess credentials.
- English proficiency if the admitting degree was not taught in English (all components in one sitting).
- No GRE.

MEng is **self-funded**. Admission assumes you can cover tuition and living costs. Full-time students may apply for paid TA roles. Domestic full-time tuition is on the order of ~$15k CAD/year; international full-time ~$63k CAD/year (2026–27 posted figures; confirm on Student Accounts). EFT roughly halves the annual rate but you pay two years.

### File they actually read

1. **Transcripts** for every post-secondary institution, with grading scale, including at least one term of final-year grades.
2. **CV.**
3. **Letter of intent, 650–1000 words.** Official prompt: why this program; research/professional interests; career goals; **courses you intend to take**.
4. **Two references:** two academic, or one academic + one professional. Academic referees must be undergraduate course instructors/professors (not workshop instructors). Institutional email required. No friends/family.

### What “fit” means for MEng (read between the official lines)

They are not asking for a supervisor or a publication record. They are asking whether you:

- Understand that this is a **professional, course-based** degree and can name a customized path through their catalog.
- Have a credible reason MIE (not a generic data-science master’s, not ECE, not iSchool) is the right department.
- Can handle quantitative coursework. The mid-B floor is real; DAML’s first course is a programming-and-math filter.
- Will use the degree to **stand out for industry jobs**, which is the sentence they use to define the MEng.

For **co-op**, they add: limited seats, “exceptional candidates,” strong academics, job-search readiness, possible interview, apply early (rolling).

Non-engineering backgrounds are welcomed **when the applicant can point to courses, emphases, or research**. A UX + statistics file should therefore look like: empirical/quant foundation already present; human-centered systems experience already present; MEng supplies the missing IE technical stack (data science methods, decision systems, optional healthcare/HF depth, professional/business layer).

Do **not** write “I am not qualified for the jobs I want.” Write the same fact as a gap the program is designed to close: the undergrad gave human-centered methods and statistical literacy; it did not confer a recognized technical graduate credential in analytics or industrial engineering.

## How this maps onto a UX + statistics background

Industrial engineering at MIE is already a three-way field: **people (human factors), information (data/AI/systems), and decisions (OR, healthcare, business)**. That is why IE feels “broad” in a useful way. The application should pick a lane through that breadth, not celebrate breadth for its own sake.

**The coherent lane**

UX + stats → **human-centered data and decision systems**, with healthcare as a plausible application domain.

| What you already have | What MIE calls it | What the degree adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| User research, task flows, prototyping, evaluation | Human Factors / HCI content in `MIE1401`, field methods, HFI | Engineering framing: workload, error, automation, safety-critical systems |
| Statistics minor, data analysis | Empirical methods, statistical learning | `APS1070` + `MIE1626` / `MIE1413`; scalable/cloud/ML electives |
| Interest in business regardless of major | ELITE | Leadership, project management, product/consulting-shaped electives |
| Want roles that currently screen out a UX-only credential | DAML alumni titles: data scientist, BI analyst, healthcare analyst, ML engineer | Transcript emphasis + MEng (Eng) credential |

DAML’s own application list includes **adaptive user interfaces**. That is the official bridge sentence between a UX background and their analytics emphasis. Use it.

**What not to do**

- Do not apply as a mechanical engineer in training (thermofluids, manufacturing, robotics) unless that is actually the goal.
- Do not pick robotics or advanced manufacturing as emphases “because they sound like MIE.”
- Do not treat Healthcare Engineering or Information Engineering as the two *emphasis* selections; they are certificates. Put them in areas of interest.
- Do not propose two copies of the same data course (`1624` and `1626` exclude each other).
- Do not make ELITE the whole story. They still require a majority of technical courses.

**Letter of intent spine (650–1000 words, do not paste the form questions)**

1. **Origin:** UX + statistics trained you to study people empirically and to treat data as evidence, not decoration.
2. **Limit:** that combination is enough to start analysis work, not enough to be hired or trusted as a data/decision professional. You need graduate-level methods and an engineering credential.
3. **Why MIE, specifically:** IE is the department that already joins human factors, information engineering, and applied machine learning. Faculty include psychologists and computer scientists, and the department says non-ME/IE undergrads are normal. Name Human Factors and Applied Statistics / Cognitive Engineering / healthcare HF if you have looked at labs.
4. **Plan:** DAML + ELITE; list 6–8 concrete courses (APS1070, 1626, 1401, 1413 or 1416, 1513 or 1517, APS1001, one more ELITE). Mention optional project or Fall 2027 co-op only if you will actually pursue it.
5. **Outcome:** roles they already claim for alumni — data scientist, healthcare analyst, BI analyst, product-adjacent technical roles — in healthcare, government, or product organizations. You are not asking them to invent a new career path.
